# Treating keyword themes as a market portfolio

## Why

FirstHomeBayArea's own product principle is "start narrow, expand only after meaningful usage is demonstrated" (San Jose first, then Santa Clara, Milpitas, Sunnyvale, Campbell). The same discipline applies to keyword themes before it applies to geography: rather than one blended campaign, I'm running three keyword themes ([`../campaign/README.md`](../campaign/README.md)) as if they were three small markets in a portfolio, and letting the data — not intuition — decide which one earns more budget.

This is the same judgment a role managing a portfolio of accounts or campaigns makes: not "is this thing performing," but "which of these things should get more of a limited budget, and why."

## Decision rule

After the first two weeks (even budget split), reallocate: the theme with the lowest cost-per-real-conversion *and* a stable (not just lucky-small-sample) conversion rate gets a larger share of week 3–4 budget. Theme C stays funded at a baseline level regardless, because it's the control group that tells me whether A/B's advantage is real or just a smaller, cherry-picked audience.

## Where this goes next

If this were scaled to a real growth team's budget, the same table extends horizontally into geography (Santa Clara, Sunnyvale, Milpitas) and channel (Google Search → Meta → LinkedIn, if targeting a B2B audience). The mechanism — rank a portfolio by a real unit economic, not by raw volume — doesn't change.
